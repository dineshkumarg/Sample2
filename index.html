Cat B — 2026 Feasibility (MQ-Based Model, v5, Cat B Growth Now Cascades)

23 MBBS colleges. Built directly from Management Quota data (2025 Final Merit List, 2025 college-wise allotments, and the 2026 Final Merit List) instead of the earlier CQ/qualified-list-based approach. Non-local (NL) candidates are excluded throughout, since they're not real competition for local seats. New in v5: fixed a real gap found through direct questioning — each college's own Cat B seat growth previously eased only its own closing, with no propagation to weaker colleges below it in the ranking. Now a college's own Cat B growth (not just Cat A growth) feeds into the cascade chain, so easing at one college can genuinely displace candidates into the next-weaker one, same as it already did for Cat A. A downward-only cascade (top colleges easing weaker ones below them) is the only direction that's logically sound here — a weaker college gaining seats doesn't make a stronger college above it easier to get into, since that stronger college's own candidate pool is unaffected by seat changes elsewhere. Defaults changed to None (0%) for both sliding dropdowns — the cascade mechanism, while mechanically reasonable, isn't validated against real historical outcomes (only one year of seat-growth data exists), so the page now opens on the most conservative reading: real seat growth only, with none of the speculative cascade/sliding effects applied unless you deliberately choose to add them.

What changed from the earlier CQ-based version

  • Competing pool is MQ 2026 Provisional (local/OU only, 3,760), not the full qualified list. Non-local (NL) candidates are excluded from both 2025 and 2026 MQ data throughout.
  • Reference pool for historical patterns is MQ 2025 Final Merit List (local only, 3,631), not TGQualified2025.
  • No separate "seats increase" toggle — seat growth is always applied, using the latest seat matrix.
  • No government-college-only cascade — replaced by a Cat B→Cat A sliding dropdown, combining a general historical MQ/CQ pattern with a college-specific Cat A seat-growth cascade (see below for why these two were combined into one dropdown rather than kept as separate always-on effects).
  • Minority & Women colleges sliding is back as its own dropdown, using the corrected 60%/21.25% Cat A/Cat B split for those 4 colleges (not the 50%/29.75% used for regular colleges).
  • All India Quota (MCC) allotments are not factored in for now, per explicit instruction — a candidate who already has a confirmed MCC seat is still counted as part of the Cat B competing pool here.
  • Three dropdowns total: MQ joining rate (+1% to +5%, default +3%), Cat B→Cat A sliding (0–100%, default 25%), Min/Women colleges sliding (0–100%, default 25%).
How this MQ-based model is calculated

The competing pool

  • Start: MQ 2026 Provisional Merit List, 7,052 candidates.
  • Keep only Local (OU) status — 3,760 candidates. Non-local (NL) candidates are dropped entirely, since they're not competing for these seats.
  • Reference pattern: MQ 2025 Final Merit List, local only — 3,631 candidates, re-ranked by score.

Percentile mapping (same method as before, new pool)

  • Each college's real 2025 phase-closing position (as a rank out of the 3,631-strong 2025 local MQ pool) is converted to a percentile.
  • That percentile is relocated within the 3,760-strong 2026 local MQ pool to find the equivalent 2026 score.

Seat growth (always applied, no toggle)

  • Each college's own 2025→2026 Cat B seat growth, from the latest seat matrix, extends its closing position using its own local join density.
  • All 23 colleges' 2025→2026 seat growth now comes directly from the seat matrix, including SVS (Mehboobnagar), RVM (Siddipet), and Mediciti (Ghanpur) — these were initially missed due to spelling variations in the source file, since found and corrected.

MQ joining rate dropdown (+1% to +5%, default +3%)

  • Reflects year-over-year variation in how many offered candidates actually join, based on prior years' patterns.
  • Higher settings mean more of a college's own rank window actually joins, which tightens (not loosens) the projected closing slightly, since fewer additional candidates are needed to fill the same growth.

Cat B→Cat A sliding dropdown (combines two mechanisms deliberately)

  • Why this became one dropdown instead of two automatic mechanisms: an earlier version of this page applied two effects separately, both always-on at full strength: (1) a general historical rate of top MQ scorers also getting a CQ seat anywhere, and (2) a college-specific cascade from real Cat A seat growth. Both describe the same underlying phenomenon — a Cat B aspirant instead getting a Cat A seat — and applying them separately, both at full strength, risked counting the same displaced candidates twice. For Apollo specifically, the combined effect at full strength moved its closing by more than its entire original position. That's a real risk, not a hypothetical one, so the two are now combined into a single number per college, with one slider controlling how much of it applies.
  • Mechanism 1 (general): using the complete real 2025 MQ/CQ pattern (not just 2026's partial CQ Phase 1 data), identified the top ~10.6% of MQ 2025 local candidates and checked how many also got a CQ seat anywhere that year. The rate varies sharply by standing — roughly 60–75% for the very top few percent, tapering to 0% at the boundary — carried across years via S.No/percentile position, not by reusing a raw score.
  • Calibration check added: re-checked this same top ~10.6% group against CQ's Phase 2 allotment specifically (not just the final result) — and found the exact same 92 candidates overlap at both checkpoints. In other words, 100% of this group's eventual movement to CQ had already happened by Phase 2, with nothing further occurring in Phase 3. This confirms the 24% overall rate behind Mechanism 1 is a stable, fully-settled number, not an artifact of waiting for final data — consistent with the general pattern that Cat A allotments are mostly settled by Round 2, with little further movement to Round 3.
  • Mechanism 2 (college-specific): each college's own genuine Cat A seat growth (the seat matrix's real increase for 2026-27, not the gap between 2025's ~80% fill rate and 2026's full design capacity, which would overstate things) eases that same college's own Cat B pool. Only 5 colleges have real growth (Mamata Bachupally, SVS, Bhaskar +25 Cat A seats each; Maheshwara, MNR +50 each). Government colleges' own real growth (~41 new open seats across 4 colleges) doesn't ease any college directly, but feeds into Apollo as the natural first stop, then chains down — if Apollo's easing reaches into the next-best college's range, that college eases too, and so on.
  • 0% (None) ignores this combined effect entirely. 25% (default) applies a quarter of it — the conservative reading, given the overlap concern above. 100% applies the full combined effect as originally computed, with no discount.
  • Cascade order (for mechanism 2's chain) is fixed at each college's own baseline, un-eased position, not re-sorted after easing — letting the order shift produced a real bug during development (a weaker college briefly displaced Apollo as the anchor), caught and fixed before this shipped.

Minority & Women colleges sliding (Shadan, Ayaan, Deccan, Dr. VRK Women's)

  • These 4 colleges admit only minority/women students via Management Quota, and are excluded from the feasibility table itself (see "Other notes" below) — they're not among the 23 colleges shown.
  • Real 2025 joiners across all 4: 114. Corrected 2026 capacity (these colleges use a 60% Cat A / 21.25% Cat B split, not the 50%/29.75% used for regular private colleges): 138.
  • This dropdown estimates how many candidates who'd otherwise compete for one of the 23 listed colleges instead get absorbed into these 4 minority/women colleges, based on real overlap with each listed college's own historical range.
  • Same reasoning as the Cat B→Cat A dropdown above: 25% is the default, a conservative reading, with 100% available for the full modeled effect.

Feasibility rating (decided by S.No position, not score)

  • Scores aren't reliably comparable across years, since exam difficulty varies — the same raw score can represent very different competitive standing in different years.
  • Your S.No is first converted from your raw published position (1–7,052, which mixes local and non-local candidates) to your local-only standing (1–3,760, ranked among local candidates only) — this local standing is what actually drives the comparison, shown in the confirmation line under the input box.
  • Each college's projected P1/P2/P3 cutoffs are likewise expressed as local S.No thresholds, not scores — converted from the real 2025 pattern through the same percentile scaling used everywhere else in this model.
  • Scores are still shown in the table for readability, but the actual Very Safe/Safe/Borderline/Very Risky decision compares S.No position directly: a smaller S.No is a stronger position.
  • Very Safe — your local S.No clears P1 (toughest, smallest threshold). Safe — clears P2. Borderline — only P3 would take you. Very Risky — your S.No is larger (weaker) than P3's threshold.
